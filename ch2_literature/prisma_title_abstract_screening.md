### Key Observations

#### Dominant Assumptions Identified

1. **Privileged Access Assumption:** Many observability solutions (especially eBPF-based) assume kernel-level or privileged container access, which is incompatible with non-privileged CI/CD container environments. While eBPF offers low overhead, it requires privileges that are typically unavailable in constrained deployments.

2. **Application Instrumentation Requirement:** A significant portion of distributed tracing studies (OpenTelemetry-focused) require application code modification or SDK integration, making them unsuitable for third-party CI/CD services (GitLab, Jenkins, SonarQube, Nexus, Dependency-Track) that cannot be modified.

3. **Homogeneous Environment Assumption:** Most studies evaluate observability in Linux-only or Kubernetes-native contexts. Very few explicitly address heterogeneous OS environments (Linux + Windows) or the constraints of on-premise infrastructure without orchestration control.

4. **Service Mesh Prevalence:** A large cluster of papers focuses on service mesh architectures (Istio, Linkerd, Envoy). These solutions introduce sidecar overhead and typically target microservices architectures rather than monolithic third-party CI/CD services deployed as containers.

5. **Cloud-Native Bias:** The majority of literature assumes cloud-provider infrastructure with elastic resources and managed Kubernetes. Few studies address the constraints of fixed on-premise hardware, limited administrative access, or the absence of cloud-native APIs.

#### Gaps Relevant to Constrained CI/CD Observability

- **Non-Privileged Container Observability:** Limited research on telemetry collection from application-level containers without host or kernel access.
- **Third-Party Service Instrumentation:** Few studies address observability of unmodifiable, third-party applications deployed in containers.
- **Heterogeneous OS Support:** Minimal focus on observability architectures that span Linux and Windows hosts in the same deployment.
- **On-Premise Constraint Awareness:** Most solutions assume cloud-managed infrastructure; guidance for fixed hardware, limited privileges, and no orchestrator control is scarce.
- **CI/CD-Specific Reliability Metrics:** While many studies measure MTTD/MTTR in general contexts, few focus on CI/CD pipeline reliability or correlate observability with pipeline throughput and developer experience.

#### Research Opportunities

- **Application-Level Telemetry Exporters:** Development of sidecar or agent patterns that extract metrics, logs, and traces from containerized CI/CD services without modifying their code or requiring host privileges.
- **Cross-Platform Correlation:** Techniques for correlating telemetry signals across heterogeneous OS environments (Linux and Windows) in unified observability pipelines.
- **Low-Overhead Telemetry Pipelines:** Comparative evaluation of push vs. pull telemetry architectures to minimize resource overhead (target <5%) in constrained environments.
- **Reproducible Reference Architectures:** Creation of open-source, reproducible observability frameworks tailored to on-premise, non-privileged, third-party CI/CD deployments, with clear guidance on trade-offs (data fidelity, retention, overhead).
